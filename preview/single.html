How to pick a power bank that actually lasts

August 1, 2026 · Editorial Desk

The number printed on a power bank is the capacity of the cells inside it, not the charge it can hand to your phone. Between the step-up converter, the cable and the heat lost along the way, a 10,000 mAh pack realistically delivers somewhere around 6,500 mAh.

What the rating actually means

Cell capacity is measured at 3.7 V. Your phone charges at 5 V or higher, so the pack has to convert, and conversion is never free. A well-built unit loses about a third; a cheap one loses closer to half.

If two packs claim the same capacity and one costs a third as much, the difference is almost always in the cells and the conversion circuit.

What to look for

  • Rated output in watts, not just amps
  • USB Power Delivery or Quick Charge support, matched to your phone
  • A stated cell brand
  • Pass-through charging if you want to charge the pack and phone together

Weight is the honest signal. Lithium cells have a known energy density, so a pack claiming 20,000 mAh that weighs 200 g is not holding 20,000 mAh.

A quick comparison

RatedRealisticTypical weight
5,000 mAh~3,300 mAh110 g
10,000 mAh~6,500 mAh210 g
20,000 mAh~13,000 mAh400 g

Buy for the output you need and the weight you will actually carry. Browse power banks once you know which of the two matters more to you.
